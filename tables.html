    Tables¶

    Every table in jplacer works the same way: the parts, packages and their pads, boards, panels and their placements, the job's boards, feeders and vision settings. You can sort it, choose rows, move, widen and hide its columns, and add fields of your own to what its rows are.

    Sorting, choosing and copying¶

    Click a column's heading to sort by it; click it again to turn it round. Clicking another heading sorts by that first and by the earlier ones after it (up to three); the later ones' arrows are fainter. Choose several rows with Shift+click and Ctrl+click; Ctrl+A chooses them all, and Ctrl+C copies the chosen rows, a tab between cells (in the columns' order as shown).

    Search¶

    Search, at the right of a toolbar, shows only the rows with the text typed anywhere in them, in any column, a hidden one's too, whatever its case. The text is a regular expression: ^C0402 finds rows with something starting C0402. Beside it, while it searches, how many rows it leaves of how many (12 of 340), and in each cell what it matched is marked. A row found only in a column the table does not show has a mark at its start, and pointing at it says which column it was found in. The ✕ at its right end empties it.

    A column's heading and a colon first searches that column alone: lcsc: C15 finds the rows whose LCSC starts with or holds C15, and lcsc: alone the rows with anything in it. As you type the start of a column's heading, the rest of it shows greyed after what you typed; Tab (or → at the end) takes it, with the colon. A colon after something that is no column's heading (http://) is part of the search.

    Moving and widening columns¶

    Drag a heading's edge to widen a column; the columns after it give way. Drag a heading sideways to move its column. While it is dragged the table shows how it will look: the column floats with the pointer and the others move aside to make its place. Letting go keeps it there; Escape puts every column back as it was. Each table keeps its columns' order, widths and sort as you leave them, the next time jplacer starts too. They are kept by what each column is, not by its heading, so a column you rename keeps its place.

    Views¶

    A view is a table's layout kept under a name: which columns it shows, their order and widths, and its sort. Each table has its own, and switching between them changes the whole layout in one click: a simple view, a supplier's, a production view, your own. Each starts with two: Standard, the table as jplacer ships it, and All Fields, every column, always (those you add later too; its ticks in Columns are greyed, as it hides none). Whatever you change in a view (a column shown or hidden, moved, widened, a sort) is kept in that view as you change it: there is nothing to save.

    Beside Columns on the toolbar, each view has a button showing its icon, its name in its tooltip; the view shown is lit. Click one to show it, or press Ctrl+1 to Ctrl+9 in the table for its first nine (the keys can be changed in Edit ▸ Preferences…, as Show View 1 to 9). Drag a button along the row to move its view there. Right-click it for its own menu:

    Entry
    Show "name" Shows it.
    Rename… Gives it another name.
    Icon The icon its button shows: List, Columns, Star, Truck, Wrench, Eye, Person, Clipboard, Tag, Flag, Box, Gear or Check.
    Duplicate A copy of it beside it (as shown now, changes and all), shown.
    Locked Keeps the view as it is: a change while it is shown lasts only until another view is shown, its button marked with a dot meanwhile. Unlocked while changed, the change is its own.
    Keeps Its Search The view keeps the search typed while it is shown, and puts it back in Search whenever it is shown again: a production view of feeders showing only those enabled, a view of the parts with no height.
    Revert View (the view shown) Puts it back as it was kept, the changes since it was shown undone.
    Delete View Takes it away (a table keeps one view at least).

    Columns¶

    Columns (the table icon), at the end of a toolbar's icons after a divider, starts with the table's views: each one (the one shown ticked, (changed) after a locked one changed), New View… (a view made from the one shown, named as you say, with an icon no other view has yet) and This View, the view shown's own menu. Then it chooses which columns the table shows: a tick for each column shown; click one to hide it or to show it again in its place, at the width it had. The last column showing cannot be hidden. Reset Columns at the bottom shows them all again, as they first were. Which columns are shown is kept with the rest of the table's layout, each table its own. The tables on the Job, Boards, Panels, Parts, Packages, Vision, Feeders and Issues & Solutions tabs have it and their views, the footprint's pads too, and the table a feeder's Part or a part's Package drops has them beside its filter (its views its own, though no fields are added or edited from it, as it is only chosen from). A table with no view buttons (those in a window of their own) is a plain table: its Columns and its headings' menu show and hide its columns, and offer no views and no fields to add or edit.

    Right-click a heading for that column's menu: Hide "name", Show in Every View (shown in each of the table's views, not only the one shown), Add Field After "name"…, Edit Field "name"… and Reset Columns.

    Editing many rows, pasting and undo¶

    A value typed into a cell, a tick or a choice, in a row that is one of several chosen, goes into every row chosen: choose 50 parts, type LCSC in one row's Supplier, and it is in all 50. Choose the rows, then type (or press F2) in the cell, tick it, or pick its choice; a double-click in one of the chosen rows edits them all too, where a plain click on one chooses it alone. A row that cannot take the value (one whose cell cannot be changed) is left as it is; a value refused by one row is taken by none, the cell staying open to put it right. The status line says how many were changed.

    Ctrl+V pastes what Ctrl+C copied, or a block of cells copied from a spreadsheet (a tab between cells, a line a row): from the chosen cell, down the rows and along the columns as the table shows them. One value goes into every row chosen. A cell that cannot be changed is passed over, and the status line says how many. A value a cell cannot take (a word for a number, a choice it does not have) and nothing at all is pasted, the first few refused named.

    Each change is one step Edit ▸ Undo (Ctrl+Z) takes back and Edit ▸ Redo (Ctrl+Y) makes again, however many cells it changed, named for it ("Undo Change Rank of 12 Placements", "Undo Change 3 Cells of 2 Rows"). The library's tables (parts, packages and their pads) are undone as the library's changes are (see Parts); the others' steps are kept with Machine Setup's and the library's, the last made first, the last 200 of them.

    Fields¶

    Each column of a table of things (parts, placements, feeders…) is one of their fields. jplacer has its own, the columns each table has always had, and you can add as many of your own as you want: an LCSC part number, a supplier, a reel's size, a customer's order number. A field belongs to the kind of thing, not to one table: a field added to placements is a column of the Job tab's placements, a panel's fiducials, and every other table of placements. And the import offers each one to fill (see Importing a board).

    Add Field…, in Columns and on a heading's menu, adds one. Give it a Name (unique among that kind's fields, and without a colon), a Type, and the Import matches: the headings of a CAD file's or BOM's column that the import takes for it, commas between (its name always counts, and upper and lower case, spaces and punctuation do not matter). It shows in the table you added it from, at the end or after the column whose heading you right-clicked, and is hidden in the other tables of its kind until you tick it in their Columns.

    The type says how its values are typed and shown:

    Type Typed as Shown
    Text anything as typed
    Number a number (12, -0.5, 12,5) as typed, on the right, sorted as numbers
    Length a length in any units (1.2, 40mil, 0.5in); without units, the System Units in the System Units, sorted by length
    Yes/No a tick box
    Choice one of its Choices (commas between when you make it) picked from its list; the empty one at the end takes it out

    A value that is not one of its type is refused, and the cell stays open to put it right. Emptying a cell takes its value out.

    Edit Fields… (in Columns) lists every field of that kind: its name, its type (jplacer's own for its fields), whose it is, whether jplacer needs it, its import matches, and how many have a value in each of yours. Edit…, or a double-click, or Edit Field "name"… on a heading, opens the field: rename any field, and change the import matches of one an import fills; a field of yours also its type and choices. Changing a field of yours to another type reads its values again as that type. Those it cannot read are named first, and emptied once you agree.

    Delete… takes a field of yours away with its values (how many have one is said first). A field of jplacer's you have no use for is Removed…: it leaves every table, the import and the search, its values staying as they are (jplacer uses some of them itself), until Restore in Edit Fields… brings it back. The fields jplacer cannot work without have no Delete…:

    Table of Kept always
    Parts ID, Height, Package
    Packages ID
    Pads Name, X, Y, Width, Length, Rot.
    Placements ID, Part, Side, X, Y, Rot.
    Boards and panels where they lie Board/Panel Id, Side, X, Y, Z, Rot., Enabled?
    Boards, Panels Board Name, Panel Name
    Feeders ID, Name, Part, Type, Enabled
    Vision settings Name

    The fields are kept in fields.json in the configuration folder. Each thing keeps its own values with it: a part's, a package's and its pads' in the library, a placement's and a board's in the board's file, a panel's in its file, the job's boards and the job's own in the job, a feeder's in feeders.json, vision settings' in vision-settings.json. A placement's values are its board's: on the Job tab, a board used more than once keeps no values of its own, so there they are shown and not changed.

    Job fields¶

    A job has no table of its own, so its fields are in Job ▸ Job Fields…: a row for each field added to jobs, its value changed in place, and Add Field… and Edit Fields… as every table has them. The job's values are kept in its file.
